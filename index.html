Reed Knapper Ratings (RKR)

How I built the model

RKR started as a spreadsheet and became a full model that refits itself every week. Here's how it turns raw data into a projected line for every game.

Collect the data

Games, betting lines, play-by-play efficiency, Elo, roster talent and returning production from CollegeFootballData.com, going back to 2014. Every backtest uses only what was known before kickoff.

Rate every team

Each team gets opponent-adjusted offense and defense ratings for success rate, explosiveness, rushing and passing efficiency, line yards, stuff rate and short-yardage success, updated after every game, plus a margin-of-victory rating and Elo.

Learn from the market

A market rating built from past closing spreads teaches the model what Vegas already knew. A travel factor, faster in-season updates and a preseason starting point from talent round it out.

Fit the weights

A ridge regression on a decade of games decides how much each piece matters, refit season by season so the model is always tested on games it never saw. In the portal era, recent seasons count more.

Turn edges into picks

A pick only counts when the model's line beats the opening line by enough: 4+ points in power-conference games, 4–6 in Group of Five games, and unders 5+ below the total. No official picks in Weeks 1–3 or bowls, and a big line move against a pick drops it to a lean.

Re-check every hour

Lines refresh hourly from 10 sportsbooks until kickoff, and a live in-game model built from 6,400+ games drive by drive updates during games. Each Sunday the ratings refit and next week's projections post.

The build

From spreadsheet to live model

The spreadsheet

The first version lived in a Google Sheet. It produced picks, but an honest audit showed the best bets were basically a coin flip, and bigger edges weren't winning more often.

Rebuilt on a decade of history

I started over with a historical dataset going back to 2014 and a walk-forward backtest, so every rule had to prove itself on seasons the model hadn't seen.

Rules that survived testing

Only bet at the opener, shop the best line, unders only on totals, and treat huge Group of Five edges as model error. Ideas that didn't hold up in both test periods were thrown out.

v3.1: learning from the market

Adding a market rating and travel made the model's lines sharper. Live lines, props, upset alerts and an in-game model were built on top of it.

v3.4: built for the portal era

Rosters flip way faster now. Teams went from about 6 incoming transfers each in 2021 to 22 in 2026, and returning production keeps dropping. So I re-tested the model on 2022–26 only. What mattered: giving recent seasons and recent games more weight, and trusting returning production less early in the year. What didn't: portal rankings, transfer QBs and first-year coaches added nothing the model wasn't already seeing. In that test v3.4 went 231-145 at the opener, up from 219-161, with smaller misses in 4 of 5 seasons. That's the version running today.

Track record

The numbers, honestly

What the testing says

  • 347-225 (60.7%) in the 2021–26 backtest at the opening line, across spreads and unders.
  • When a pick qualified at the opener, the market moved toward the model 58% of the time.
  • On seasons the rules weren't tuned on (2017–19, against the closing line), it went 144-138 (51.1%). That's the realistic floor, and why I track closing-line value every week.
